How Your MSL Score Works

Measures of Student Learning • Cañon City Schools Fremont RE-1

How the old RANDA scoring worked

In the old RANDA method, each measure was given one of three ratings using cut points set separately for that measure. Each rating was worth a fixed share of the measure's points:

Less Than ExpectedNo points
ExpectedHalf the points
More Than ExpectedAll the points

Where you landed within a rating didn't change your score. The new calculator keeps the same 300-point scale, rating ranges, and RANDA entry, but replaces the three fixed results with a sliding scale. Hitting your Goal still earns half the points, and every point between Min and Max moves your score.

How the new calculator works

Your Goal Score is the midpoint, not the finish line.

1
Reach your Goal and you earn half of that measure's points. That's the "Expected" mark.
2
Reach your Max and you earn all of that measure's points.
3
Every point in between counts. Points rise steadily from Min to Max, so each point of improvement earns credit.

Example: a 20% measure

Your MSL score is 30% (300 points) of your Final Effectiveness Rating. This can be split between collective and individual measures. A 20% measure is worth 200 points.

Goal Score85
Max Score100
Min (auto)70
Score achieved92

Min sits the same distance below Goal as Max sits above it: Min = 2 × Goal − Max = 2 × 85 − 100 = 70

Points earned for this measure 146.67 / 200.00

Why isn't 92 worth 92%?

The scale for this measure runs 70 → 100, not 0 → 100. A score of 92 is 22 of the 30 points up that scale:

(92 − 70) ÷ (100 − 70) = 73.3%
73.3% of 200 points = 146.67

Scoring against 0–100 would ignore your goal. Someone who hit exactly their goal of 85 would get 85% instead of the intended 50%, and the goal would no longer be the midpoint.

How it adds up to 300

Each measure is scored out of its own points: Weight ÷ 30 × 300. The measure scores are added together for your MSL Score.
